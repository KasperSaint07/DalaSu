# 02. Evidence of Relevance

**Project:** DalaSu
**Date:** 2026-10-23
**Status:** Pre-defense 1. Verified findings and assumptions are listed separately in section 5.

## 1. Purpose and summary

This document records what evidence supports the relevance of DalaSu and what it does not yet prove. It uses two sources:

| ID | Source | Type | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | Online survey "DalaSu — опрос фермеров о поливе" (Google Forms), 8 responses | Survey of potential users | Done |
| E2 | Comparison with existing systems ([04-existing-systems-comparison.md](04-existing-systems-comparison.md)) | Desk research | Done |

Individual interviews with user representatives have **not** been held yet, and there is no agreement with a client. The survey is not presented as a substitute for either (see section 7).

Main findings from E1, all self-reported by 8 respondents:
- Irrigation timing is decided mainly by feel and experience: soil by touch (5 of 8), plant condition (4 of 8), fixed schedule (3 of 8), weather forecast (3 of 8). Nobody selected soil sensors or an agronomist's advice.
- The most frequent difficulty is water availability (6 of 8 selected the option about water being available only at certain times; the option text is truncated in the form view).
- A recommended irrigation date is the most wanted feature (7 of 8), followed by a recommended amount of water (5 of 8).
- All 8 respondents rated the proposed tool as "very useful" (3) or "rather useful" (5).

## 3. Survey results

### 3.1 How irrigation is decided today

Multiple choice, n = 8.

| Option | Count | Share |
|---|---|---|
| By soil condition, by feel | 5 | 62.5% |
| By plant condition | 4 | 50% |
| By a pre-set schedule | 3 | 37.5% |
| By weather forecast | 3 | 37.5% |
| When access to water appears | 2 | 25% |
| By sensor readings | 0 | 0% |
| By agronomist's advice | 0 | 0% |

**How the amount of water or duration is decided (free text).** I could read 7 of the 8 answers in the form view. Six describe experience, visual assessment, soil and plant condition or weather, and say the volume is not measured exactly (examples: "we do not measure exactly, by eye"; "I orient on experience... I do not measure the exact volume of water"). One answer says watering continues until the tree-trunk circle is full, or the water amount is measured by time. One answer is just "Да".

### 3.2 Difficulties in the last season

Multiple choice, n = 8. Some option labels are truncated in the form view and are quoted as shown.

| Option | Count | Share |
|---|---|---|
| "Вода была доступна только в…" (truncated) | 6 | 75% |
| "Почва пересыхала или была…" (truncated) | 3 | 37.5% |
| Water was insufficient | 2 | 25% |
| Hard to determine the needed amount ("Было сложно определить нуж…") | 2 | 25% |
| No significant difficulties | 2 | 25% |
| Hard to determine the time ("Было сложно определить вре…") | 1 | 12.5% |
| It started to rain after watering | 1 | 12.5% |
| Watering took a lot of time ("Полив требовал много време…") | 1 | 12.5% |

**Cases where irrigation went wrong (free text, n = 8).** Four answers describe no such case or are not informative ("Не было", "Таких случаев не было", "Таких ситуации не было на этом поле", "Дапрогноз погоды"). The other four describe concrete cases:
- Tomatoes were watered in the evening, heavy rain fell at night, the soil was waterlogged and the next watering had to be postponed.
- In July water reached the canal two days later than scheduled during heat of about +38 °C.
- On hot days the soil dried faster than usual, the watering was insufficient, tomato leaves began to wilt, and watering had to be extended, which cost extra water and time.
- Water is not always available, it is hard to determine the needed amount, and sometimes rain follows watering.

### 3.3 Freedom to change date and amount

| Answer | Count | Share |
|---|---|---|
| Yes, can choose independently | 5 | 62.5% |
| Partly, there are limitations | 2 | 25% |
| No, depends on the water supply schedule | 1 | 12.5% |

Six restriction comments were readable (7 answers exist). Three say that water comes by a canal schedule set by the water user: the date can only be shifted slightly, the water reserve lasts a short time, and the volume depends on how much water reaches the plot. One says the watering dates depend on water availability and the schedule cannot always be changed. Two say there are no restrictions or that the respondent changes the schedule and duration with weather and plant condition.

### 3.4 Current tools

Free text, n = 8. All 8 rely on personal experience, a weather forecast on the phone, visual assessment of soil and plants, or the water supply schedule. No respondent names a dedicated irrigation tool. Statements about gaps (translated from Russian):
- "The forecast is not always accurate. There is not enough information about soil moisture and the necessary volume of water."
- "It would be good to have sensors for tracking soil moisture."
- "I would like to have a simple tool that would suggest the optimal time and amount of watering."
- "It would be convenient to have an app that suggests when and how much to water."

### 3.6 Opinion about DalaSu

**Usefulness to the respondent personally (n = 8):** very useful 3 (37.5%); rather useful 5 (62.5%); rather not useful, not useful and "not enough information" 0.

**Most useful features, up to three (n = 8).** The label of the second option is truncated in the form view ("Рекомендуемое количество в…").

| Feature | Count | Share |
|---|---|---|
| Recommended irrigation date | 7 | 87.5% |
| Recommended amount of water | 5 | 62.5% |
| Accounting for expected precipitation | 3 | 37.5% |
| Irrigation history | 2 | 25% |
| Working with unstable internet | 2 | 25% |
| Reminders about watering | 1 | 12.5% |
| Explanation of the recommendation | 1 | 12.5% |

**What respondents need to see before trusting recommendations (free text; I could read 7 of 8).** Three answers ask to check the recommendations on a small part of their own plot and compare them with their usual watering. Three state criteria: accuracy, correct account of weather and soil condition, and that the app must account for soil, crop and the water supply schedule and explain why it recommends a given watering. One asks for test results, one for an online version of the model first, one says the app "must meet my expectations", and one answer ("Ла") is not informative.

**Language (n = 8):** both equally convenient 5 (62.5%); Russian 2 (25%); Kazakh 1 (12.5%).

## 4. Evidence from existing systems

[04-existing-systems-comparison.md](04-existing-systems-comparison.md) shows that sensor-based tools (CropX) charge for hardware and subscriptions, and that Kazakhstan already has the free state application AgroData and the commercial platform Egistic. This supports the sensor-free positioning, but also means that DalaSu cannot claim to be the only affordable option.

## 6. Implications for requirements (proposed)

These are the team's proposals based on the evidence. They are for discussion with the supervisor and do not change [06-requirements.md](06-requirements.md) by themselves.

| Evidence | Proposed implication |
|---|---|
| 7 of 8 want a recommended date, 5 of 8 an amount | FR-07 is the central user need; consider raising its priority from Should |
| Respondents do not measure volume | Check with farmers in which unit the amount should be shown (depth in mm, volume or duration) |
| 6 of 8 report water availability difficulties; 3 of 8 can only partly or not change the date; respondents ask the app to account for the water supply schedule | Consider a new requirement: let the user enter the water supply windows, and build recommendations inside them |
| 3 of 8 want expected precipitation accounted for | Keep precipitation in the water balance (FR-06) and show it in the recommendation |
| 4 of 8 do not have stable internet on the plot | Revisit the offline-first question in [05-technology-comparison.md](05-technology-comparison.md); at minimum show cached recommendations with their age |
| Respondents ask for an explanation of the recommendation and for accounting for soil and crop | Supports NFR-07 and FR-05, currently Could and Should |
| 5 of 8 are comfortable with both languages | Supports FR-13; the interface should offer Russian and Kazakh |
| Respondents want to test on a small part of the plot and compare with their usual watering | Add a side-by-side comparison with the farmer's usual practice to the verification plan (09) |

## 7. Limitations

- **Sample size and selection.** 8 respondents who chose to answer. Three locations point to the south of Kazakhstan, and the rest are elsewhere. The survey cannot be generalised to southern small farms.
- **Self-reported and hypothetical.** Usefulness ratings concern a product that does not exist yet. All 8 ratings were positive, but this may reflect politeness or selection, and half of the sample was not ready to discuss further.
- **Data quality.** One area answer has no unit ("13"), one respondent reports 1000 ha, and one reports cows rather than crops. These answers are not used for conclusions about farm size or crops. Some free-text answers are uninformative ("Да", "Ла", "Дапрогноз погоды").
- **Reading of the form.** Some option labels are truncated in the form view, and not every free-text answer was visible. Counts come from the chart view of the Responses tab.
- **No interviews or client agreement yet.** The checklist asks for 3–5 discussions with user representatives or a substantive agreement with the actual client. This document does not claim either.
